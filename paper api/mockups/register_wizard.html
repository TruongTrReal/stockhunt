In five minutes

Three calls: register a strategy, mint a key, send an order. The wizard on your desk does the first two and hands you the third with your ids already in it.

1  POST /v1/strategies   → 201  {"strategy_id":"st_7Q2M4K","state":"pending"}
2  POST /auth/keys       → 201  {"key":"sk_live_…"}   session only, shown once
3  POST /v1/orders       → 202  {"seq":1,"state":"accepted"}   queued, not filled

Authentication

One header, every call: Authorization: Bearer sk_live_…. A browser session works too, but a key is what unattended code should carry. A key cannot mint another key — that is deliberate, and it is why revoking is final.

Idempotency

Every order carries a client_order_id you choose. Sending the same one twice returns the first order with 200 instead of creating a second. Derive it from something stable — strategy, symbol, bar timestamp. If you derive it from a clock, a retry after a timeout becomes a doubled position, and you will not see it until somebody reconciles the book by hand.

Order lifecycle

you ──POST──▶ accepted ──desk pass──▶ working ──▶ filled
                  │                              └──▶ partial
                  └──────────────────────────────────▶ rejected  (reason set)

202 is the left-hand box and nothing more. Everything after it happens on the desk's schedule, and reason is where a refusal explains itself.
